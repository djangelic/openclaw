## [THE STORY] ~2 minutes

[VISUAL: Angel talking to camera, relaxed but focused.]

So here's what happened. Part of my job at n8n is writing scripts — video scripts, demo scripts, presentation scripts. And I was using ChatGPT for all of it. Every day. It was my co-writer.

And the scripts were... fine. They were fine. That's the problem. "Fine" is the enemy of good when you don't have a second opinion.

[CUT TO: Screen recording showing side-by-side script comparison, names blurred]

Then one day I switched to Claude. Not for any dramatic reason — I was just testing it out. And the difference was *immediate*. The scripts got sharper. Less filler. More voice. My voice, not "helpful AI assistant" voice.

[CUT TO: Angel back on camera]

And I realized — ChatGPT had been making me *worse* and I didn't even notice. Because when you use the same tool every day, you lose the ability to judge it. You just... adapt to the output.

Now here's the part that matters. The switch was *easy*. Like, embarrassingly easy. And the reason it was easy is because I'd already built all my tools as MCP servers running through n8n.

[SCREEN RECORDING: Quick flash of n8n workflow with MCP nodes]

The AI was just one node in the workflow. I swapped that node. Everything else — my context, my memory, my integrations — all stayed the same. The plumbing didn't care which brain was plugged into it.

But imagine if I'd gone all-in on ChatGPT plugins. Custom GPTs. ChatGPT-specific memory. I'd *still* be writing fluffy scripts right now, because the switching cost would've been too painful. I would've stayed — not because it was better, but because leaving was too expensive.

[BEAT — looks directly at camera]

That's not a tool. That's a trap.

## [THE WORDPRESS ANALOGY] ~1 minute

[B-ROLL: Quick montage of WordPress sites, hosting dashboards, cPanel]

I've been running a web design firm for 25 years. Over 200 clients. Almost every single one of them is on WordPress.

[CUT TO: Angel on camera]

And you know why WordPress won? It wasn't because it was the best CMS. It won because you could *leave*.

You could export your content, move to a new host, and be up and running overnight. That portability is what made people trust it enough to stay.

[VISUAL: Angel gesturing, getting more animated]

AI tooling is at that exact same inflection point right now. And MCP — the Model Context Protocol — that's the portability standard. That's the thing that lets you swap the brain without rewiring the body.

The platforms that lock you in? They're Squarespace. They're Wix. They're the thing that feels easy until the day you need to leave.

## [THE iOS ANGLE] ~1 minute

[SCREEN RECORDING: iPhone showing iOS Shortcuts app with AI integrations]

Same pattern shows up on your phone. iOS Shortcuts is basically n8n for your iPhone. And right now, I have shortcuts that call ChatGPT, shortcuts ready for Gemini when Apple opens that up, and Apple's own local model built right into the OS.

[CUT TO: Angel on camera, slightly amused]

Now — the local model? It chokes on anything longer than a grocery list. It's not great yet.

But here's the thing — the *shortcut* doesn't care which model it calls. The shortcut is the tool layer. The AI is just the engine underneath.

[VISUAL: Angel counting on fingers]

Phone. Desktop. Server. Three different environments, probably three different AI models. But the same tool layer across all of them. And for me, n8n is that constant. It's the plumbing that doesn't change when the AI does.

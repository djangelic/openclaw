## [THE PROBLEM] ~1.5 minutes

[VISUAL: Angel on camera, storytelling mode]

Let me paint a picture you've definitely seen before. Someone on your team — maybe it's you — champions a new tool. You research it, you pitch it, you get buy-in, you roll it out. Training sessions. Onboarding docs. Slack messages saying "hey everyone, please start using this."

[B-ROLL: Montage of SaaS dashboards, login screens, empty Kanban boards]

Three months later, two people use it. Everyone else went back to Slack messages and Google Sheets.

[CUT TO: Angel on camera]

That's the Tool Graveyard. And roughly 80% of enterprise software ends up there. Not because the tools are bad — they're usually fine. They fail because they're not where people already work.

And here's the part nobody talks about: every tool you champion that fails? That's *your* credibility on the line. You're the person who made everyone sit through that training. You're the person who said "this will change everything."

[VISUAL: Angel leans in]

So the insight — and it took me way too long to figure this out — is dead simple. Tools succeed when they meet people where they are. And your team? They're in Slack eight hours a day. That's where they live. That's where they work. That's where your tools need to be.

## [THE LAST LAYER THESIS] ~1.5 minutes

[VISUAL: Angel stands up, moves to a whiteboard or uses a simple graphic]

The Last Layer is an idea. It's the idea that Slack should be the universal enterprise UI.

[CUT TO: Simple diagram — apps on one side, Slack in the middle, users on the other]

Not another dashboard. Not another login. Not another tab you forget exists. Slack as your security layer, your audit layer, and — most importantly — your adoption layer. Because adoption is the only metric that actually matters.

[CUT TO: Angel on camera, getting animated]

Let me give you a real example. We worked with a company that had a security scanner — BlackCloak. Powerful tool. CLI-based. The kind of thing where you open a terminal, type a command, get results.

[SCREEN RECORDING: Brief flash of a CLI tool, then a Slack bot interface]

Guess who used it? Engineers. Only engineers. Because nobody else was going to open a terminal.

We moved it into Slack. Same tool. Same backend. Just a different front door.

[CUT TO: Angel on camera, snaps fingers]

Now sales reps run security scans on live client calls. They type a slash command, get results in seconds, and the client sees it happen in real time. Same tool — ten times the users, and honestly? Better security posture across the whole company. Because the tool is *where people already are*.

That's The Last Layer.

## [THE AI BOUNCER] ~1.5 minutes

[VISUAL: Angel back at desk, slightly more serious tone]

Now let's talk about the elephant in every enterprise meeting room. AI.

AI is powerful. You know that. I know that. Your CEO definitely knows that because they read about it on LinkedIn.

But here's what's embarrassing — access control for AI is basically nonexistent right now. Most companies give their AI models blanket access to everything. Every document, every database, every system. And then they wonder why security is nervous.

[B-ROLL: Abstract visual of data flowing unrestricted, then a bouncer at a door]

Here's how I think about it. n8n acts as a bouncer. It sits between AI and your systems and controls exactly what AI can see and exactly what it can do. Not suggestions. Hard limits.

[CUT TO: Angel on camera]

And here's the beautiful part — the same bouncer works at every layer. Desktop, phone, server. You can swap the AI model underneath and the access controls don't change. Today it's Claude, tomorrow it's Gemini, next month it's something we haven't heard of yet. The bouncer stays.

[VISUAL: Angel uses hands to illustrate]

Think of it like a contractor. You hire a contractor to remodel your kitchen. You give them a key to the front door. You do *not* give them your master key that opens every room in the building, your filing cabinet, and your safe.

But that's exactly what most companies do with AI right now. Full access, hope for the best.

The Last Layer is about putting the bouncer in place *before* you let AI through the door.
